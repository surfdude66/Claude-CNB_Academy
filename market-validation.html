Singapore's hands-on cooking and baking market is real and active — but it is a single-session market: 2–4 hour classes priced roughly S$90–250 dominate every roundup and studio found [1][2][3]. Cook & Bake's core bet is structured 1–8 week progressions, a format almost no competitor runs at scale — only two studios found offer anything past a single day [3]. Our per-session-equivalent pricing (S$160–245, from catalogue fee ÷ session count) sits at the upper edge of that market, which is defensible if buyers value the structured, skill-ladder format. What public sources cannot answer is whether Singapore buyers will actually commit to weekly attendance across 2–8 weeks at these prices — that is the one question this report leaves open, and the one a real market-research step (landing page waitlist or intent survey) should close before the website build.

Who is likely to pay

Persona 1

The Skill-Ladder Hobbyist

28–45 · working professional

Treats cooking as a structured hobby to progress in, the way others treat a fitness programme — wants technique, not just a recipe, and will pay for a multi-session arc that ends in a real skill.

Evidence: several Singapore studios now run SkillsFuture/WSQ-subsidised multi-session programmes — Grá Baking Academy, BELLS Baking Studio's 8-day workshops, Pots & Pans — signalling real demand for credentialed, structured hobby learning [3].

Persona 2

The Experience Gifter

25–38 · buying for a date, gift, or self-treat

Books a single low-commitment session as a birthday gift, date-night activity, or one-off indulgence — price-sensitive on time, not on money.

Evidence: cooking and baking workshops appear repeatedly in Singapore "date ideas" and "creative workshop" roundups as a distinct experience-gifting category, not just a class [9][8].

Persona 3

The Team-Bonding Buyer

HR / office manager, booking for 10–70 pax

Books on behalf of a company for an offsite or appreciation event — a distinct B2B channel with a higher per-booking value than any single consumer sale.

Evidence: at least seven dedicated cooking team-building providers operate in Singapore, charging S$80–150 per person for a 2–3 hour session [4].

Demand & seasonality

6–20 Oct 2026

First intake wave — clear of school holidays

Falls after the Sept school break and well before year-end holidays [7] — a clean launch window with no competing family-calendar pull.

21 Nov – 31 Dec 2026

MOE year-end holidays (41 days) + Christmas

Matches most of our December intakes. This is the natural window for the gifting persona and the ages-12+ courses (Cupcake & Cake Pops, Cookie Baking) [7].

6–7 Feb 2027

Chinese New Year — currently unaddressed

CNY goodie demand (pineapple tarts, bak kwa-style bakes) typically peaks in the 4–6 weeks prior [6]. None of the 20 courses have an intake or curriculum in the mid-December to late-January window aimed at this — a catalogue gap, not a launch blocker.

30 May – 28 Jun 2026 & 2027

Mid-year school holidays (30 days)

A second family/teen window for the ages-12+ workshops. Current intakes don't reach this far — worth an intake for the next planning cycle [7].

Year-round

Corporate bookings — no confirmed seasonality

Sources describe steady demand for team-building cooking sessions with no stated seasonal pattern; some providers anecdotally note a Q4 pickup around year-end appreciation events, but a magnitude figure is UNKNOWN [4].

The five to launch first

Chosen for breadth across all three personas, a low-commitment entry point into the format Singapore's market doesn't already sell, and coverage of both campuses — deliberately deferring the premium multi-week anchors (French Pastry, Wedding Cake, French Culinary Foundations) to a second wave once initial demand is confirmed.

CUL-210 · Knife Skills & Kitchen Essentials

The lowest-friction trial product in the catalogue — a single session that converts into every other course. Fits the Hobbyist as a first step and the Gifter as a low-risk present.

Risks

Unverified

Multi-week commitment is a novel ask

The market we found is built on single 2–4 hour sessions [1][2][3]. Whether Singapore buyers will commit to 2–8 weekly sessions at S$160–1,580 is not answered by any public source — it needs a direct demand test before the website spend.

Internal calc

Thin margin below ~70% fill

At 12 seats, BAK-101 (4 sessions, S$680) grosses S$8,160 per cohort before instructor, ingredients, campus overhead. A single under-filled cohort swings the unit economics fast — worth modelling against actual cost inputs before pricing is finalised.

Missing input

Two campuses, fixed cost from day one

No public source gives typical Singapore culinary-studio rent per square foot, so a realistic breakeven fill rate for Orchard Road and Bukit Timah is UNKNOWN pending an actual lease-cost figure from the founder.
